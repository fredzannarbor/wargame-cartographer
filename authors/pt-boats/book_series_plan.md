# The Harwick PT Boat Library — 12-Book Series Plan

**Publishing cadence:** One book every 2 months (12 books over 24 months)
**Realistic adjusted cadence:** One book every 3–6 months with AI-assisted research and drafting

Each entry below includes: title, subtitle, scope, why it fills a gap, estimated word count, key primary sources, and photo source strategy.

**Essential baseline reference:** Bulkley's *At Close Quarters: PT Boats in the United States Navy* (1962) — the official Navy history, available in full text at ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/CloseQuarters/. Every book in this series must surpass Bulkley's treatment of its subject in depth, narrative quality, and primary-source diversity.

## Book 1: PT BOATS IN THE MEDITERRANEAN — The Forgotten Mosquito Fleet

**Subtitle:** *American Motor Torpedo Boats from North Africa to Southern France, 1942–1945*

**Scope:** The complete history of 42 American PT boats across three squadrons fighting a two-and-a-half-year war in the Mediterranean, from MTBRon 15's first patrol out of Bone, Algeria (April 28, 1943) to the final offensive naval patrol of the Mediterranean war (April 28–29, 1945). Covers the North African coast campaign, the Sicilian and Italian invasions, the Anzio beachhead, Corsica-based interdiction of Axis coastal shipping, the invasion of Elba, and Operation Dragoon (Southern France). A theater where American PTs operated under British Coastal Forces command in joint patrols—a unique inter-Allied arrangement found nowhere else in the PT boat war.

**Why it fills a gap:** No single volume has ever covered the full Mediterranean PT story from start to finish. Bulkley's *At Close Quarters* (Part VI) dedicates chapters but is spread across a 500+ page comprehensive history. The Mediterranean PTs fought a fundamentally different war than their Pacific counterparts: primarily Higgins 78-ft boats (not Elco 80-ft), closely integrated with British MTBs and MGBs, engaging German F-lighters (170-ft, 120-ton cargo vessels with 88mm guns) and E-boats rather than Japanese barges. By war's end, Mediterranean PT units fired 354 torpedoes, claimed 38 vessels sunk (23,700 tons), lost four boats, and suffered five officers and 19 men killed. This compact, intense campaign has never received standalone treatment.

**Key Squadrons:**
- **MTBRon 15** (PT-201 through PT-218) — Pioneer squadron, arrived January 1943 under Lt. Comdr. Stanley M. Barnes. Primarily Higgins 78-ft boats. First American PT squadron in the European theater.
- **MTBRon 22** (including PT-305 "Sudden Jerk" and PT-309 "Oh Frankie!") — Arrived May 1944. Based on Corsica, later St. Tropez. 77 offensive patrols.
- **MTBRon 29** — Arrived May 1944, Elco boats. Joined Squadrons 15 and 22 for the final push up the Italian coast.

**Key Operations:**
- Bone, Algeria patrols (April 1943) — Barnes personally took PT-206 to Cape Bon and torpedoed a German tanker
- Operation Husky — Sicily (July 1943) — dispatch boats, escorts, nightly patrols
- Salerno (September 1943) — smoke screens, anti-shore battery fire, shot down German planes
- Anzio beachhead defense (January–May 1944) — the "dummy landing" deception using loudspeakers and fireworks
- Joint British-American patrols (November 1943–April 1944) — 14 engagements, 15 lighters sunk, two E-boats, a tug, an oil barge
- Invasion of Elba (June 18, 1944) — PT-305 sank a German Flak lighter
- Bastia and Calvi, Corsica — forward operating bases for Ligurian Sea interdiction (35 miles from the Italian mainland)
- Operation Dragoon — Southern France (August 1944) — RON 22 landed French Commandos, diversionary force in Gulf Juan, anti-E-boat screen
- Final coastal campaign up to Leghorn (Livorno) and the Franco-Italian border

**Base progression:** Bone (Algeria) → Bizerte (Tunisia) → Palermo (Sicily) → Salerno (Italy) → Maddalena (Sardinia) → Bastia/Calvi (Corsica) → St. Tropez/Golfe-Juan (France)

**Estimated length:** 90,000 words + 50 photographs + 6 maps

**Primary sources:**
- Action reports, RON 15/22/29 war diaries (NARA RG 38)
- COMNAVNAW (Commander Naval Forces Northwest African Waters) operational records
- British Coastal Forces Mediterranean records (UK National Archives, ADM series)
- Oral histories from PT Boats Inc. archive
- Italian and German naval records for the adversary perspective (German records confirm loss of six small ships of destroyer types, 600–900 tons, to U.S. PT attacks)
- *At Close Quarters* Part VI as baseline (available via HyperWar)

**Photo strategy:** NARA 80-G series (Mediterranean subsection), NHHC NH series, **National WWII Museum Joe Cirlot collection** (PT-305/RON 22 crew photos from Corsica and Leghorn), British Imperial War Museum for combined operations photos, Italian Navy historical archive (Ufficio Storico della Marina Militare) for Axis perspective shots.

## Book 2: BUILDING THE MOSQUITO FLEET — Elco, Higgins, and the Industrial Race to Arm the PT Navy

**Subtitle:** *Design, Construction, and Production of America's Motor Torpedo Boats, 1938–1945*

**Scope:** The complete industrial and engineering history of PT boat design and construction. From the 1938 design competition through the wartime production surge. Covers all three primary builders—Electric Launch Company (Elco) of Bayonne, NJ; Higgins Industries of New Orleans, LA; and Huckins Yacht Corporation of Jacksonville, FL—plus the experimental and one-off designs. Includes the British Power Boat Company influence, the Packard marine engine story, and the evolution from 77-foot Elco boats to the definitive 80-foot Elco and 78-foot Higgins designs.

**Why it fills a gap:** Frank J. Andruss Sr. covered the factual/technical construction story across three books (*Building the PT Boats*, *PT Boats Behind the Scenes*, *Pieces of History*), but his work is reference-oriented, not narrative. No book has told the PT boat construction story as a dramatic industrial narrative—the Elco-vs-Higgins rivalry, Andrew Jackson Higgins's political maneuvering and congressional lobbying, the engineering challenges of planing hulls at 40+ knots, the human drama of wartime production under impossible deadlines. This book is to Andruss what *Freedom's Forge* (Arthur Herman) was to the dry production statistics of WWII industry.

**Estimated length:** 85,000 words + 60 photographs + engineering drawings

**Primary sources:**
- Elco company records (held at various archives)
- Higgins Industries records (National WWII Museum, New Orleans)
- Bureau of Ships (BuShips) correspondence and specifications (NARA RG 19)
- Packard Motor Car Company marine engine division records
- PT boat acceptance trial reports
- Congressional testimony on small combatant procurement

**Photo strategy:** NARA industrial/shipyard photos, National WWII Museum Higgins collection, Mystic Seaport Museum for early small craft photos, factory floor images from the Bayonne and New Orleans yards.

## Book 3: SCHOOL OF THE BOAT — Training America's PT Crews at Melville

**Subtitle:** *The Motor Torpedo Boat Squadrons Training Center, 1942–1945*

**Scope:** The untold story of how the Navy trained 1,797 officers and 11,668 enlisted men across 44 squadrons at the Motor Torpedo Boat Squadrons Training Center (MTBSTC) in Melville, Rhode Island — the only PT boat training facility in the United States. Established February 17, 1942, under Lt. William C. Specht (trainees called it "Specht Tech"), the base grew to 59 buildings and 150 Quonset huts with a boat basin literally carved from a swamp. Covers the three-month training course (one month aboard a PT boat), gunnery training at the Anti-Aircraft Center at Price's Neck, Newport, the transition from peacetime Ivy League yachtsmen to wartime draftees, and the training mishaps that make great stories (PT-122 nearly sank after straying into a torpedo test range; PT-59 accidentally launched a torpedo that traveled seven miles and hit the cargo ship USS Capella). Notable alumni included JFK (entered September 27, 1942, completed December 2), plus a future Supreme Court justice, three movie stars, five ambassadors, four NFL coaches, and a U.S. attorney general.

**Why it fills a gap:** Melville has never been the subject of a dedicated book. Every PT boat crew that went to war passed through this small Rhode Island town, yet the training experience is at most a paragraph in existing works. The cast of characters alone — from JFK to future movie stars and Supreme Court justices — would sustain a book, and the story connects to broader themes: how the Navy improvised a training pipeline for an entirely new type of warfare from scratch in months.

**Estimated length:** 75,000 words + 40 photographs

**Primary sources:**
- MTBSTC administrative records and training syllabi (NARA)
- Class rosters and graduation records
- Personal papers of instructors and students (PT Boats Inc. archive)
- Melville town historical records
- Training films and manuals ("Know Your PT Boat," 1945)

**Photo strategy:** NARA training facility photos, PT Boats Inc. archive (personal snapshots from trainees), Melville town historical society, Navy training film stills.

## Book 4: THE WEAPONS THAT EVOLVED — PT Boat Armament from Torpedoes to Rocket Launchers

**Subtitle:** *How the Mosquito Fleet Reinvented Itself at Sea, 1941–1945*

**Scope:** The dramatic transformation of the PT boat from a single-purpose torpedo craft to a multi-role gunboat bristling with weapons. Standard armament began with 2–4 Mark 8 torpedoes (2,600 lbs, 466-lb TNT warhead), twin .50 cal M2 machine guns, and 20mm Oerlikon cannons. Crews then scavenged 37mm M4 cannons from crashed P-39 Airacobra fighters on Guadalcanal — so effective they were later factory-installed. JFK's PT-109 had an Army M3 37mm anti-tank gun lashed to the bow. 40mm Bofors guns (120 rpm) became standard additions. By war's end, two eight-cell Mark 50 rocket launchers firing 5-inch spin-stabilized rockets (11,000-yard range) gave PT boats 16 rockets plus 16 reloads — matching a destroyer's 5-inch broadside. Raytheon SO radar (17 nm range) provided decisive advantage in night engagements. The result: by 1945, PT boats had more firepower per ton than any other vessel in the U.S. Navy. Also covers torpedo tubes replaced by lightweight racks, engines upgraded from 1,100 hp to 1,500 hp, depth charges, naval mines, and smoke generators.

**Why it fills a gap:** The weapons evolution story is one of the most technically fascinating aspects of PT boat history, yet it exists only in fragments across other books. The PT boat's transformation was driven by the practical failure of torpedo tactics against shallow-draft targets (10-ft minimum depth settings against barges drawing 5 ft) and the discovery that PT boats were far more effective as close-range gunfighters and interdiction platforms. The field modification story — crews literally pulling guns off crashed aircraft — is one of the great improvisation narratives of the war.

**Estimated length:** 80,000 words + 55 photographs + technical diagrams

**Primary sources:**
- Bureau of Ordnance reports on PT boat weapons (NARA RG 74)
- Field modification reports from squadron commanders
- Torpedo performance analysis reports
- Radar installation and performance records
- Mark 50 rocket launcher development records
- After-action reports documenting weapons effectiveness

**Photo strategy:** NARA ordnance testing photos, NHHC weapons detail shots, PT Boats Inc. archive for crew-modified weapons photos, Aberdeen Proving Ground historical photos for weapons development.

## Book 5: GREEN HELL BOATS — PT Operations in New Guinea, 1942–1944

**Subtitle:** *The Motor Torpedo Boat War Along the World's Most Hostile Coast*

**Scope:** The comprehensive history of PT boat operations along the New Guinea coast, from the first boats based at Tufi in late 1942 through the final operations along the Vogelkop Peninsula in 1944. Covers the unique challenges of operating in equatorial waters with coral reefs, tropical disease, extreme heat, and Japanese barge traffic. Features the key actions at Lae, Salamaua, Finschhafen, and the Vitiaz Strait.

**Why it fills a gap:** New Guinea PT operations were among the most sustained and grueling of the war, yet they are overshadowed in existing literature by the Solomons campaign and the PT-109 story. The barge-busting war along the New Guinea coast was where PT boats truly found their role as coastal interdiction platforms, and where many of the tactical innovations later used throughout the Pacific were first developed.

**Estimated length:** 85,000 words + 45 photographs + 8 maps

**Primary sources:**
- RON 7, 8, 12, 18, 21, 24, 36 war diaries and action reports (NARA RG 38)
- Seventh Fleet PT boat operational records
- Australian War Memorial records (PT-Australian Army cooperation)
- Japanese convoy and barge movement records (captured documents)

**Photo strategy:** NARA 80-G series (South Pacific subsection), Australian War Memorial photo collection, PT Boats Inc. archive for New Guinea-specific images.

## Book 6: RETURN TO THE PHILIPPINES — PT Boats in the Liberation Campaign, 1944–1945

**Subtitle:** *From Leyte Gulf to Manila Bay: The Motor Torpedo Boats' Longest Fight*

**Scope:** PT boat operations during the Philippines liberation campaign, from the initial landings at Leyte in October 1944 through the end of hostilities. Covers the Leyte Gulf actions (including PT boats' role in the Battle of Surigao Strait), the Mindoro and Lingayen Gulf operations, the Manila Bay campaign, and the final operations in the southern Philippines and Borneo approaches. This was the largest concentration of PT boats in any single campaign.

**Why it fills a gap:** While the 1941-42 Philippines PT story (the *They Were Expendable* era) is well-known, the 1944-45 return campaign—which was far larger in scale and more operationally significant—has never received dedicated book-length treatment. More PT boats served in the Philippines liberation than in any other campaign, and their contributions to shore bombardment, convoy escort, and barge interdiction were substantial.

**Estimated length:** 95,000 words + 50 photographs + 10 maps

**Primary sources:**
- Seventh Fleet PT operational summaries (NARA)
- RON war diaries for all Philippines-assigned squadrons
- Battle of Surigao Strait action reports (PT boats were the first to engage the Japanese Southern Force)
- MacArthur's headquarters records on coastal operations
- Japanese naval records for adversary perspective

**Photo strategy:** NARA 80-G Philippine operations series, NHHC Leyte Gulf collection, MacArthur Memorial Archive (Norfolk, VA).

## Book 7: NIGHT FIGHTERS — PT Boat Tactics and the Art of Small Craft Combat

**Subtitle:** *Doctrine, Tactics, and Combat Leadership in the Motor Torpedo Boat Squadrons*

**Scope:** A tactical analysis of how PT boats actually fought. Covers the evolution of doctrine from pre-war torpedo attack theory to wartime reality: night ambush tactics, radar-directed attacks, the "barge sweep" pattern, coordinated operations with aircraft (Black Cats), shore bombardment techniques, and the increasingly sophisticated multi-boat tactical formations developed by experienced squadron commanders. Includes case studies of exemplary and failed engagements analyzed from both sides.

**Why it fills a gap:** Existing PT boat books are primarily narrative histories of specific units or campaigns. No book has systematically analyzed PT boat tactics as a military subject. How did commanders plan and execute night attacks? What radar and communication procedures were used? How did tactics differ between theaters? This book bridges the gap between popular narrative and professional military analysis.

**Estimated length:** 80,000 words + 40 photographs + 20 tactical diagrams

**Primary sources:**
- "Motor Torpedo Boats: Tactical Orders and Doctrine" (1942, 1943, 1944 editions)
- Commander Motor Torpedo Boats Pacific Fleet tactical bulletins
- After-action reports with tactical analysis
- Enemy assessment of PT boat tactics (Japanese and German records)
- Interviews and papers of key tactical innovators

**Photo strategy:** NARA tactical photos (night action shots are rare but exist), radar scope photographs, tactical diagram reproductions from official doctrine manuals.

## Book 8: THE BOATS THAT SERVED TWICE — PT Boats After the War

**Subtitle:** *Foreign Navies, Coast Guard Service, Korean War, and the Surviving Boats*

**Scope:** What happened to America's 800+ PT boats after V-J Day. Overseas boats were stripped and burned — 121 boats destroyed at PT Base 17 on Samar, Philippines alone. Boats returned to the U.S. were sold for trivial sums (PT-305 sold for $10 in 1948). Civilian conversions ranged from tour boats and fishing charters to oyster boats and yachts — PT-615 became a yacht leased to Clark Gable ("Tarbaby VI"); Howard Hughes purchased PT-694, later used in the McHale's Navy TV show. Covers the 146 boats built specifically for Lend-Lease export (the Soviet Navy received approximately 90 Vosper 70-ft types, Higgins 78-ft, and 60 Elco 80-ft boats — these Western designs influenced postwar Soviet torpedo boat development). Ends with the preservation stories of surviving museum boats: PT-305 "Sudden Jerk" (National WWII Museum, restored and seaworthy), PT-309 "Oh Frankie!" (Fredericksburg, TX), PT-617 and PT-796 (Battleship Cove — PT-796 was repainted as PT-109 for JFK's 1961 inauguration), PT-658 (Portland, OR, fully operational, National Register of Historic Places since 2012), and PT-728 (Liberty Aviation Museum, the only surviving U.S.-built Vosper design).

**Why it fills a gap:** The post-war story of PT boats is essentially untold. Most readers assume the boats were simply scrapped (many were), but the full story involves Cold War Lend-Lease politics, Soviet reverse-engineering of American designs, celebrity civilian owners, and a preservation movement that has saved a handful of boats for future generations. The contrast between $10 surplus sales and multimillion-dollar restorations decades later is a story about how a nation remembers — and forgets — its wars.

**Estimated length:** 80,000 words + 50 photographs

**Primary sources:**
- Navy disposal records (NARA)
- Foreign military aid records (State Department and Navy)
- Korean War coastal operations reports
- PT Boats Inc. records on the preservation movement
- Interviews with restoration volunteers (PT-658 crew)

**Photo strategy:** NARA post-war disposal photos, foreign navy archives, PT-658 Heritage Museum collection (Portland, OR), Korean War-era Navy photos.

## Book 9: THE SHADOW MISSIONS — PT Boats in Intelligence and Special Operations

**Subtitle:** *Agent Insertion, Coastwatcher Support, and Clandestine Warfare*

**Scope:** The classified and little-known role of PT boats in intelligence and special operations throughout the war. In the Mediterranean, Squadron 15 conducted multiple OSS missions landing agents on the west coast of Sardinia and northwest coast of Sicily; OSS operatives used PT boats to reach Capri, where they met sympathetic Italian admirals and anti-fascist scientists and learned about German radio-guided bombs and Italian magnetic torpedoes. Corsica — 35 miles from the Italian mainland — became a departure point for fast-boat infiltration of SO and SI agents into central and northern Italy. In the Pacific/Burma theater, the OSS Maritime Unit's "Flotilla Officer Commander" conducted 36+ missions across the Bay of Bengal, ferrying swimmer teams and rescuing 220+ downed airmen. The OSS Maritime Unit pioneered swim fins, face masks, and closed-circuit diving equipment — laying groundwork for modern Navy SEAL operations. Also covers coastwatcher evacuation in the Solomons and Lt. Comdr. Murray Preston's Medal of Honor action using PT-363 and PT-489 to rescue a downed aviator under fire in Wasile Bay off Halmahera Island.

**Why it fills a gap:** This is essentially virgin territory. The intelligence and special operations role of PT boats is mentioned in passing in a few books but has never been systematically researched. PT boats' speed, shallow draft, and ability to operate close inshore at night made them ideal platforms for clandestine missions, and they were used far more extensively in this role than the public record suggests.

**Estimated length:** 75,000 words + 35 photographs + maps

**Primary sources:**
- Declassified OSS records (NARA RG 226)
- Coastwatcher service records (Australian and New Zealand archives)
- Allied Intelligence Bureau records
- British SOE records for Mediterranean operations (UK National Archives)
- Personal accounts from PT crews involved in special missions

**Photo strategy:** NARA intelligence/OSS photo collections, Australian War Memorial, limited operational photos (the nature of these missions meant few were photographed), supplemented with location photos and equipment shots.

## Book 10: BASES AND TENDERS — The Logistics of the Mosquito Fleet

**Subtitle:** *How the Navy Kept PT Boats Fighting from Tulagi to Cherbourg*

**Scope:** The untold logistics story: how the Navy established, supplied, and maintained PT boat bases across two oceans. Centers on the PT boat tenders (AGP classification) — USS Niagara (AGP-1), USS Oyster Bay (AGP-6) and the Oyster Bay-class vessels (modified Barnegat-class, 2,500 tons, 311 ft, capable of operating in 12 feet of water, carrying 48 replacement torpedoes with expanded engine repair shops). Covers the Seabee-built advanced bases, the hull construction that enabled field repair (two diagonal layers of 1-inch mahogany planking with canvas and glue between, held by bronze screws and copper rivets — specifically chosen for repairability), parts shortages and field-expedient repairs, fuel supply challenges for the three Packard engines consuming enormous amounts of high-octane gasoline, and the 1,200-mile self-powered PT movement from New Guinea to Leyte Gulf — the largest such movement of the war, requiring fuel shuttles from tenders supplied by tankers.

**Why it fills a gap:** Every PT boat book mentions the bases in passing, but the logistics infrastructure that made PT operations possible has never been given dedicated treatment. This is the story of the motor machinists, shipfitters, Seabees, and supply officers who kept the boats running—often with nothing more than ingenuity and scrounged parts.

**Estimated length:** 80,000 words + 55 photographs + base layout diagrams

**Primary sources:**
- PT boat tender (AGP) deck logs and war diaries (NARA)
- Seabee battalion records for base construction
- Supply and maintenance records from forward bases
- Bureau of Ships reports on PT boat material condition
- Personal accounts from maintenance and logistics personnel

**Photo strategy:** NARA base construction and maintenance photos (well-documented in 80-G series), Seabee museum collection, PT Boats Inc. archive for informal base life photos.

## Book 11: EVERY MAN'S NAVY — Race, Class, and Service in the PT Boat Squadrons

**Subtitle:** *The Untold Social History of America's Motor Torpedo Boat Crews*

**Scope:** A social history of the men who served on PT boats. Covers the early-war recruitment of Ivy League yachtsmen and the transition to a truly democratic cross-section of American manhood. Examines the service of African American personnel in PT boat squadrons (present but largely undocumented), the class dynamics between reserve officers and career Navy men, the experience of enlisted crews, combat stress and its management, and the veterans' post-war trajectories. Includes statistical analysis of crew demographics alongside personal narratives.

**Why it fills a gap:** PT boats were one of the few Navy assignments where officers and enlisted men lived and fought in intimate proximity, creating a unique social dynamic. The racial dimension is particularly unexplored—while the Navy officially segregated most units, PT boat support personnel included African Americans whose service has been almost entirely unrecorded. This book recovers voices that have been lost.

**Estimated length:** 85,000 words + 45 photographs

**Primary sources:**
- Personnel records and muster rolls (NARA, National Personnel Records Center)
- Veterans' oral histories (PT Boats Inc., Library of Congress Veterans History Project)
- Navy Bureau of Personnel policy records on race and assignment
- Post-war census and VA records for veterans' outcomes
- Personal correspondence and diaries

**Photo strategy:** NARA crew and personnel photos, Library of Congress Veterans History Project media, PT Boats Inc. personal photo collections, African American military service collections at Howard University and the National Museum of African American History.

## Book 12: MOSQUITO FLEET — A Complete Encyclopedia of United States PT Boats

**Subtitle:** *Every Boat, Every Squadron, Every Action: The Definitive Reference*

**Scope:** The capstone reference work. A comprehensive encyclopedia covering every PT boat (PT-1 through PT-812), every squadron (RON 1 through RON 45), every major and minor action, and every major personality in the PT boat story. Organized in multiple sections: (1) boat-by-boat entries with construction, assignment, and fate; (2) squadron histories; (3) chronological action index; (4) biographical entries for key personnel; (5) technical appendices on specifications, weapons, and equipment; (6) theater-by-theater operational summaries; (7) decorations and honors.

**Why it fills a gap:** While NavSource.net provides hull-by-hull photo indexes online, and Bulkley's *At Close Quarters* is the official history, there is no single comprehensive reference volume that brings together boat histories, squadron records, and action chronologies in one place. This is the book that every subsequent PT boat researcher will need on their shelf.

**Estimated length:** 120,000 words + 100 photographs + extensive tables and appendices

**Primary sources:** All sources from previous 11 books, plus:
- Complete set of RON war diaries (NARA RG 38)
- Bureau of Ships boat construction and assignment records
- Navy Department communiqués
- Medal of Honor, Navy Cross, and other decoration citations

**Photo strategy:** Comprehensive selection from all sources, emphasizing boats and subjects not previously illustrated. NavSource.net index as a finding aid for locating NARA/NHHC originals.
